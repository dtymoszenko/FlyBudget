# Currencies

Your budget has **one currency**: your envelopes, To Be Budgeted, reports and net worth are all
counted in it. Each **account** can have its own: a euro account next to your dollar ones, a
card from home while you live abroad, cash for a trip. Its amounts stay in its currency, exactly
as your bank shows them, and your budget counts each transaction at what it was worth in yours
on its day.

If all your money is in one currency, there's nothing to set up: everything works as before.

## The budget's currency

Choose it in **Settings → Currencies**. Nothing is converted: it only says which currency your
amounts are in, so they show with the right symbol and so accounts in other currencies can be
added. A budget gets one automatically when you first give an account a currency, or connect a
bank.

You can correct it as long as no account in another currency has transactions. Changing it after
that would mean converting every amount, which FlyBudget doesn't do.

## Accounts in another currency

When you add an account, pick its **Currency**. It can be changed until the account has its first
transaction; after that, add a new account in the other currency and move the money with a
transfer.

Accounts you connect to a bank get the bank account's currency. Linking a bank account to an
existing account in another currency warns you.

Everywhere an account's own amounts appear (its register, balance, reconciling, imports) they're
in its currency. Totals across accounts are in your budget's: the sidebar, the Accounts page and
net worth add each account at today's rate, with `≈ $1,080.00` under a balance in another
currency.

### What a transaction is worth

Each transaction in another currency keeps its value in your budget's currency next to its own
amount, worked out at the exchange rate of its day. That's what the budget and income and
spending reports count, and it stays: a later change in the rate doesn't move what you spent last
month.

Your bank charged a different amount? Open the transaction and type it in **Value in USD** (or
your currency): what your card statement says it cost, fees included. **Use the day's rate**
goes back.

### Missing rates

A transaction whose day has no exchange rate yet can't be counted honestly, so it counts as
nothing until there is one, and FlyBudget says so: the budget and reports show how many are
waiting, the register marks them **No rate**, and totals name the currencies they left out. Add a
rate (below) and they're filled in. FlyBudget never quietly counts one currency as another.

## Exchange rates

**Settings → Currencies** shows the latest rate for each currency you use, and lets you type one
in: **1 GBP in EUR** for a day. A day without a rate uses the closest earlier one, up to a month
back. A rate you type in wins over a downloaded one for its day.

### Downloading rates

Turn on **Download ECB reference rates** to fetch the European Central Bank's daily rates from
[Frankfurter](https://frankfurter.dev), an open-source service. It's off until you turn it on,
because it's a request to another server; only currency codes and dates are sent, never anything
about your budget. Once on, FlyBudget checks every few hours and fills in transactions waiting for
a rate. The ECB publishes about 30 currencies; others need rates typed in.

You can point it at your own Frankfurter server under **Server**. Self-hosted installs can also
set `FLYBUDGET_EXCHANGE_RATES_URL` (see [self-hosting](/community/self-hosting)).

**Recalculate from rates** works out values again after you correct a rate. Values you typed in
and reconciled transactions stay as they are.

## Transfers between currencies

Moving money between accounts in different currencies has two amounts: what left one account and
what arrived in the other. In **Add transaction**, choose the transfer as the category and a
second field asks for the amount in the other account, filled in at the day's rate. Type over it
with what your bank shows.

Both sides of a transfer are worth the same in your budget's currency (the side in it decides),
so moving money is never income or spending, whatever the rate. Editing one side's amount leaves
the other's alone: what left isn't what arrived.

[Transfer suggestions](./transactions#transfers) also find pairs in different currencies whose
values are within 3% of each other.

## Currency difference

Money you keep in another currency is worth more or less in yours as rates move. An account in
another currency shows that next to its balance, like **+$12.40 from the rate**. It never
changes your envelopes on its own. Choose it to **Record difference**: it adds a correction worth
that much, in a category you pick (income for a gain, spending for a loss), or none to keep it
out of income and spending.

## How amounts are written

Each currency is written as at home: `1 234,56 zł` with the złoty after the amount, `$1,234.56`
before, `CHF 1'234.56` with a space. The **Number format** in [Preferences](./settings) still
decides the separators. Under **How amounts are written** you can choose another symbol or side
for any currency you use, on this device.

Amounts are typed with the currency's own decimals: yen have none (`12.5` isn't an amount in yen),
Kuwaiti dinars three.

## Good to know

- **Backups** include your currency, the exchange rates and every value. A backup from before
  currencies existed restores into your budget's currency.
- **CSV exports** add a **Currency** column and the value in your budget's currency when you have
  accounts in more than one.
- **Imports** read amounts in the account's currency. A bank statement (CAMT) in another currency
  than the account warns you.
- **Rules** compare amounts in the transaction's own currency.
